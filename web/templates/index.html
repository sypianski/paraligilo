§ II · Method

Why two modes?

Classical alignment — Gale & Church, 1993 — uses nothing more than sentence length. It is fast, free, and on a faithful classic literary translation it lands plainly correct alignments most of the time. The deterministic mode here adds anchor pre-passes (rare proper-noun co-occurrences, dates, numbers, monotonic chains) and then runs that classic DP between anchors with full m:n moves.

Where modern translators paraphrase, restructure, drop or add — where lengths diverge — multilingual embeddings catch what surface tokens can't. The AI mode adds a vector-space cost on top, re-embeds concatenated passages where m and n differ, and (optionally) asks gpt-4o-mini to plainly say yes, no, or partial for the residue that still looks ambiguous.

What you get back

A JSONL — one record per aligned pair — with original sentence indices, translation sentence indices, the move type (1:1, 1:2, 2:1, &c.), a confidence score, and the method used. Plus a sample.md of 100 stratified pairs so you can read across the bands and judge for yourself whether the result is fit for whatever you have in mind for it.

What this is for

Parallel reading apps. Bilingual EPUB construction. Language-learning decks. Translation studies. Anything that wants this sentence here to point to that sentence there, in two languages, in a structured way you can build on.
