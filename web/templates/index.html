Upload the original and its translation. You get back a JSONL mapping each source sentence to its counterpart(s), with confidence scores and m:n move types (1:1, 1:2, 2:1, …) reflecting where the translator split or merged sentences. Output expires after 24 hours.

Method

The free mode uses surface anchors — years, numbers, rare proper-noun co-occurrences chained monotonically — then a Gale-Church length-only dynamic-programming alignment with m:n moves between anchors. Classical 1993 technique, no model, no network.

The AI mode adds multilingual sentence embeddings (default text-embedding-3-large) so semantic similarity informs the cost function, then optionally asks gpt-4o-mini to plainly label residual low-confidence pairs as yes, partial, or no. About $0.10 per book paid directly to your chosen API provider.

Measured on real pairs: 94 % high+medium confidence on Sienkiewicz Quo Vadis (PL↔EN, deterministic), 86 % on Tom Segev Seventh Million (EN↔IT, AI mode). Translator faithfulness matters more than language pair.
